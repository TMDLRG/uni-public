This is the front door of a research project, and the place it asks you to begin. It says what the
project is made of. There is a group of software agents living inside a game world, a system that
films them, and a laboratory built around a model of a bacterium's motor. A fourth part exists only
to stop the other three claiming more than they measured, and the page argues that this last part is
the important one, so it puts it first.

It then walks through a short numbered sequence, because each idea is needed to follow the next. How
the system is built to be caught being wrong, and what an agent actually is. Then why there is no
reward signal anywhere, why the thing running the broadcast is an agent too, and one name that
confusingly means two different things.

It ends by saying what the project does not claim. Passing tests here are not evidence of biological
parity, of general intelligence, or of human parity, and the page says so plainly rather than leaving
it to be assumed.

This is the opening article of a public documentation site, written for someone who has just arrived
and does not know what any of it is. It describes four things built alongside each other. There is a
group of software agents living in a game world, a system that broadcasts them, and a laboratory
built around the rotary motor of a bacterium. The fourth is a control plane whose whole purpose is to
stop the other three overstating what they have measured. The article says that last one is the right
place to start, and the rest of the page follows from that choice.

The sequence it lays out is deliberate. First, the idea that the system is built to be caught being
wrong rather than argued to be right. The checks are themselves tested by introducing a defect on
purpose and requiring the check to go red. Results are reported in a small fixed vocabulary rather
than as a percentage, and failing checks are published on the site rather than left out of it.

Then, what an agent is: a reasoning process, a separate program that plays the game, and a real
player logged into a real server. The boundary between the first two is a narrow pipe, and the
article says that if you understand nothing else here, understand that line. The reasoning part
cannot see the world; it sees senses. The playing part cannot see beliefs; it receives one action.

Then, that no reward signal exists anywhere. Agents minimise expected free energy instead, and when a
new term was added to that engine it had to leave behaviour unchanged when switched off.

Then, that the thing directing the broadcast runs the same engine, with camera cuts and the birth and
death of agents among its available actions.

Then, a warning that two different parts of this system are called by the same name, which will
confuse you if nobody says it first.

It closes with a reading order and with what the site is not claiming: passing gates are not
biological parity, not general intelligence, not human parity. Where a claim is not established the
site says so rather than choosing a comfortable reading. And the working repositories stay private while most citations
do open, into a frozen snapshot published on a stated day, so what a reader reaches is that tree as
it stood then rather than as it stands now. Where no snapshot exists the citation says it cannot be
opened rather than emitting a link that would fail, on the grounds that a citation you cannot follow
is an appeal to authority. The article carries a correction to that passage, which for a time said
none of them could be opened after that had stopped being true.
