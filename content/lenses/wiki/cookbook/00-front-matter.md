This is the opening chapter of a cookbook, and it is deliberately not a recipe. It explains what the rest of the book is a recipe for, and it sets out the rules that bind every later page. The subject is a simulation — one engine of a kind that learns by predicting and acting, wired up at many scales, from a first cell division through metabolism, movement, perception and language. The page says the same thing several times so it cannot be missed: a bounded peek at a toy world, never a person.

The one thing this chapter teaches you is how to read a status label. Every recipe closes with one word from a fixed short list — proven, designed, hypothesized, or not-yet-built — and each word is defined here exactly. Nothing may be carried above its label.

It also prints the honest position without softening it: about two of eleven-plus developmental rungs are earned. The rest are designed, hypothesized, or not built at all. A separate ledger of claims, a list added to and never edited, is treated as the source of truth, and where the book and that list disagree, the book is wrong.

This chapter is the constitution and the reading guide for a cookbook, and it asserts no capability of its own. Its subject is a developmental simulation: one engine, reused at many scales, running the same loop each time — take in evidence, plan by weighing what is worth doing, act, and learn by adding counts rather than by back-propagating errors. The chapter is emphatic that this is a bounded peek at a toy world and never a description of a person, and it says that limit is stated louder under pressure, never widened.

There is a tension the chapter names out loud and then holds. It recommends the complete recipe to the very top of the ladder — assume the whole build, cook the whole meal — while telling you at every rung exactly how much is actually cooked. The rule for conflict is stated once and applies everywhere: the ledger, an append-only list of claims nobody edits, is the single source of truth, and where recipe and ledger disagree the recipe is wrong.

The kitchen rules follow. Every claim carries an evidence class and an operable falsifier, the result that would show it wrong, and lives in that four-state record. Wording moves only downward toward the measured value, never upward, including under urgency. A verdict rests on the bound that excludes the threshold rather than on a single best estimate. Bars are registered before a build, and a set of evidence kept back is touched exactly once behind a seal. A negative bound is owed only after several structurally different negatives, and mundane explanations must be ruled out first. Work on synthetic constructions is treated as diagnostic, never as capability. Learning is by counting, with a guard that forbids gradient methods inside the loop.

The four status words are then defined. Proven means a sealed, signed pass exists with the result that would sink it still live. Designed means a specification or an agreed design exists while the build is partial or unverified. Hypothesized means a mechanism has been stated with no sealed gate behind it. Not-yet-built means no engine, no run, no gate. Designs signed off in a consultation are folded in as designed and not run, and folding them in raises nothing.

A status walk then goes rung by rung. The lower rungs carry the proven label, but the losses are printed next to the wins rather than buried. A cellular lab openly loses on several modes. A metabolism result is positive as a foraging driver and recorded as negative as a building driver, with a gate left open. A heart model is marked as a toy rather than a clinical instrument, and an affect model as modelled and never felt. A perception result is carried together with two recorded negatives, and a language result is always cited with its own caveat. The self-model rung is described as functional only, with phenomenal experience disclaimed rather than tested. Reasoning and wisdom are parked. Dreaming and creativity are not built, and the awareness question is posed only as an open, falsifiable question whose answer is not known and not claimed.

A short list of red lines closes the argument: phrases about general intelligence, human-level ability, awareness, or created life are never written, and the underlying preprint is always labelled as unrefereed. Finally, the substrate work — replay, serialization, fail-closed transport, running on real hardware — is classed as engineering evidence, not evidence about the science, with its own recorded negatives carried first-class and its central coupling gap left open.
