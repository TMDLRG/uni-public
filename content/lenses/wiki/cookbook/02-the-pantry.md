A shelf, not a dish. It lists once, in one place, the engines and building blocks that every later recipe in the cookbook reaches for. When a recipe names an ingredient, you can come here to see exactly what that ingredient is, what status it holds, and what it must never be read to mean. All of it belongs to a simulation, not to a person.

The recipes do not each ship their own model. They reuse one engine and swap only the world it is coupled to. The label attached to an ingredient here travels with it wherever it is used, and a recipe inherits its ingredients' limits.

Most of the page is fences. A number computed at one level of precision may not be printed at a finer one. A reading result is a counting baseline and nothing more — not comprehension, not active inference, not a win over other systems. Hardware and substrate work is engineering evidence, never evidence that a science gate was met. Negative results are set out inline rather than tucked away. And it closes with a list of what is not claimed: no demonstration of active inference, no created life, no awareness, no human-level ability.
<!--CLEAR-->
This chapter is the stocked shelf the rest of the cookbook reaches into. It exists so that a later recipe can name an ingredient in one line and a reader can look up here what that ingredient actually is, what its status really is, and what it must not be read to prove. The load-bearing idea is stated at the top: one engine, many scales. Recipes do not each bring their own model; they reuse one discrete engine and swap only the observation model and the world it couples to.

Two governance rules travel with the food. World, body and mind are kept as separate typed layers, with signals from the hardware treated as the body's own senses. And learning is done by adding counts, with a guard that forbids gradient methods inside the loop. The chapter is explicit that these are method patterns rather than evidence, and that the language of active inference appears only as a framing lens at textbook level.

The centre of the page is a table of ingredients, each with what it supplies and its real status. There is a discrete planning engine, a higher-precision reading path, a counting reader, and a modulator that sets how strongly signals are weighted. Then a starting-point routine for a first cell division, an organ for metabolism, a motor hierarchy, a set of small browser demonstrations, and a substrate that runs the whole thing on real hardware.

Then come the fences, which take up more room than the table. The precision fence says that anchors computed at the coarser numeric setting must never be printed at the finer tier, and it records that a claim of that sort was caught and corrected. The counting fence says the reading result is a baseline win by counting and nothing more. It sits behind systems trained by gradient methods on one measure, by a chosen design trade, and it points to two published negatives attached to the same reader. The substrate fence says that engineering work on the body does not imply any science gate is met. Continuity across a real kernel swap is owed rather than shown. And coupling the body only to what has been earned is the programme's central open gap, which is parked. The affect fence says the modulator is functional and that feeling is modelled, never felt.

There is no single experiment that passes this page. Its condition is fidelity: every ingredient labelled at exactly its class and no higher, and every recipe inheriting its ingredients' fences. The falsifier is stated as its mirror image — the pantry fails if any ingredient is ever labelled above its class anywhere in the book.

Recorded negatives close the chapter, carried inline by design. The metabolism organ helps foraging and crafting but made building worse in the same live run, and a gate on that stays open. An over-compressed sensory bottleneck lost on data it had not seen, so the fuller set of channels is kept. A simplified factored posterior was built and rejected as lossy, so the exact joint version is used everywhere. The final fence repeats what is not claimed: no demonstration of active inference, no created life, no awareness, no human-level ability — a developmental simulation, a bounded peek, about two of eleven-plus rungs earned.
