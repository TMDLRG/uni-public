This chapter treats a single motile cell as an engineering problem with an unforgiving specification. The numbers all come from other people's laboratories and are cited, so this is not one of the project's own results. The vehicle is finished at launch — no transcription, no repair, no rebuild — so whatever machinery it shipped with is all it will ever have, even though fuel can be drawn from the medium along the way. The environment is viscous enough that inertia is irrelevant.

That regime is the chapter's spine, and it insists you do the arithmetic before drawing anything. At this scale a shape sequence that simply retraces itself produces no net movement at all, which rules out whole families of design. What would refute it is written out with unusual care. Exhibit one swimmer achieving sustained progress in an ordinary fluid using a strictly reversible stroke, with the fluid's behaviour measured and the obvious loopholes excluded. The chapter names in advance the escape that does not count, because a springy fluid is already inside the theory rather than a refutation of it.

From there it works through the machine. The motor slides rather than bends, and the chapter is precise about that distinction.
