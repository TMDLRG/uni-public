This is the front door of a cookbook, and it is a map rather than a recipe. The cookbook describes a simulation: one engine, of a kind that learns by predicting and acting, wired up at many different scales. The scales run from a first cell division through metabolism, movement, perception and language, and on to open questions about creativity and awareness.

This index lists the chapters in order. Beside each one sits a single honest label saying how much of that step is actually built. The labels are proven, designed, hypothesized, and not-yet-built, and they are drawn from a separate ledger of claims kept elsewhere, a list added to and never edited. The page states the rule for disagreements plainly: that list wins and the recipe is wrong.

The one thing this page says is that the whole recipe is recommended, but the evidence is not. It prints the honest position in the same breath: about two of eleven-plus developmental rungs are earned. This is a bounded peek at a toy world, never a person. Read this page first to see the shape of the book.

This page is the table of contents for a cookbook, and it is worth reading on its own because it carries the book's honesty rules alongside its list of chapters.

The subject is a simulation. The claim is not that a mind has been built. It is that one engine of a particular kind can be shown running at many scales, and that the same loop appears each time: take in evidence, choose what to do next, act, and learn by counting. The chapters walk a ladder from a molecular starting point and a first cell division, through cellular upkeep, metabolism, an organ model, affect, movement, perception, language and a self-model, and then on to reasoning, wisdom, dreaming and creativity.

The page does two things at once, and it says so. It recommends the complete recipe all the way to the top of the ladder, so a reader setting out to build should assume the full build. And it labels every rung with what is really there. Four labels are allowed and no others. Proven means a sealed, signed pass exists, with the result that would sink it still live. Designed means a specification or an agreed design exists but the build is partial or unverified. Hypothesized means a mechanism has been stated with no sealed gate behind it. Not-yet-built means there is no engine, no run and no gate at all.

Reading the table, the lower rungs carry the proven label, sometimes with recorded losses named beside the wins. The upper rungs — reasoning, wisdom, dreaming, and creativity leading to measurable awareness — carry designed or not-yet-built. Several designs that were signed off in a consultation are folded in as designed and not run, and the page is explicit that folding them in raises nothing.

Two further pointers appear. One is to a substrate chapter about the body the engine runs on, which the page marks as engineering evidence rather than evidence about the science, and it notes that a sensorium is never awareness. The other is a closing chapter on where the material came from.

The provenance note at the end names the sources the book was written from and the date a review signed off on its honesty posture. It ends with an invitation rather than a defence: no step is labelled above its evidence, no negative result is hidden, and any step may be falsified.
