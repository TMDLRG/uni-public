Air, here, is a working fluid with a density, a viscosity, a sound speed and a scattering cross-section — not a background. Every value in it was measured by a geophysicist somewhere else. Nobody here did the measuring, and nothing on the page is one of this project's own results. The closing list says what is not claimed, including any suggestion that the atmosphere is regulated, purposive or an agent.

A single property decides whether a body flies or swims, and it is not the one most people would name. Not density, and not stickiness on its own, but the ratio between them — and because air's value sits far from water's, flying and swimming are different regimes rather than one problem at two scales.

Much of the rest is a catalogue of correct laws producing wrong answers. The scattering law that makes the sky blue would, taken alone, make it violet; the missing terms are the sunlight's own spectrum and the eye's response. A rule for how sound fades with frequency holds in one limit and fails badly in real air. And a composition figure is marked as true of one month and nothing else, with a live public fact sheet quoted carrying a value decades out of date to show it.
<!--CLEAR-->
This chapter builds a specification for the gas everything else sits in, and its spine is a comparison. None of the science in it was measured here: it is published geophysics, cited, and not one of the project's own results. It closes with a list of what it does not claim, beginning with any suggestion that the atmosphere is regulated or purposive. Air and water are set side by side, and the chapter argues that the number deciding which regime a body lives in is the ratio of stickiness to density rather than either alone. Because that ratio differs greatly between the two fluids, flight and swimming are separate problems rather than the same problem rescaled. What would refute it is stated in exactly those terms. Find two geometrically similar bodies matched on the relevant dimensionless number whose flow, forces and control still differ systematically, and the spine is wrong. Compressibility is named in advance as the escape hatch a refutation must avoid.

Along the way it does the derivations. It works out how thick the atmosphere is from first principles. It treats sound, including how absorption depends on frequency and humidity. The tidy rule people quote fails in real air at everyday frequencies, because relaxation in the molecules takes over. The worked numbers show the measured change falling far short of what the rule demands, and a humidity dependence that rises then falls, which no such rule can produce. It treats light, and gives the sky's colour as its cleanest example of a correct law giving a wrong answer through a dropped term. It treats the atmosphere as a heat engine and gets a dimensionless payoff out of it. It notes that the oxygen content has a history rather than being a constant.

Two contested regions are handled at length and neither is resolved. The idea that high oxygen allowed very large insects is presented with the serious mechanism behind it, and with a recent measurement that attacks it at the delivery endpoint. The chapter is equally firm that the mirror-image overrun is inadmissible: showing one mechanism fails one test is not showing the factor was irrelevant. Print both citations or neither. Similarly, the idea that the planet's atmosphere is regulated is split in two. That the atmosphere sits far from chemical equilibrium is treated as replicated and independent of any theory. The regulation claim is marked hypothesised, with a live and unanswered objection about what could possibly be doing the selecting. The chapter adds that contempt is a defect exactly as much as credulity is, and that the limit goes on the claim rather than on the claimant.

The boundary analysis places air outside the body, as a slowly changing external state — something a body senses and is pushed by, without air modelling anything in return.

The recorded negatives include a famous folk claim about which way water turns down a drain, refuted by a dimensionless estimate rather than by a sneer. It is then rescued in the only regime where the underlying physics can win, in an experiment whose long settling time was the whole point. A published mass estimate spanning nearly an order of magnitude is marked inadmissible as a point estimate, with an instruction to quote the spread whole rather than rounding it inward. And a model showing a regulating mechanism is possible is marked as a category error when it is offered as evidence that the mechanism is running.
