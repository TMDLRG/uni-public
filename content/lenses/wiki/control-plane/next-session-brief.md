A brief for the next working session, and a plan rather than a record: it describes work to be done, and it opens by saying that this is preparation and that the following phase stays pre-registered and untouched until this completes. It also tells the reader which page to read first, for state.

The standing rules come before anything else, which tells you how they are meant to be used. Speak findings, decisions, phase edges, completion and blockage aloud, and speak an adverse result first rather than leaving it at the end. Converse rather than dump — one thing at a time, outcome in the first sentence, and if a long status block is forming it belongs on the live surface instead of in chat. Ask rather than assume, since naming, scope and contract changes are the operator's; speak the question and stop. Ground before claiming, with an unflattering count of how many times in this project a copy was reported as canonical or a document reported as truth. And read live: nothing on the live surface may be cached or hand-transcribed, and a value that cannot be read live renders as unknown rather than as a guess.

A short table records what already exists — a model of record with its containers, relationships and views; rendered exports; projections that need no tooling; the architecture prose; the decision records, each with a falsifier; the phase documents so far; and the live surface.

Four tasks follow. The first is an audit: cross-check every claim in the architecture prose, the model and the projections against the running system rather than against each other, marking each confirmed, corrected, or not verified with a reason. It lists what is known to need checking, and states its falsifier — any statement that cannot be traced to a live read or to a named file and line.

The second task is the specification that is currently absent and needed before the body is built: sequence diagrams for the evidence path and for admission, release, a two-key transition, an emergency stop and a drift and its resolution; a view one level below containers for the internals; a data specification for the ledger entry, the receipt and the renderer's node contract; every refusal in the architecture prose restated as a testable statement; and one disambiguation line that has not yet been written.

The third task is drill-down and cross-linking on the live surface: every element linking to its definition, decisions cross-linked to what they govern and back again, documents rendered in the page rather than served as raw text, each falsifier shown beside the thing it would falsify, and the interface carrying the model's parsed elements and relationships so the page can show the graph rather than only the pictures. A constraint is attached: that surface owns nothing and caches nothing, and every addition is a live read carrying the path it came from.

The fourth task is to re-render and verify, with the expected results named in advance, followed by a short list of things not to do — no new dependency, no edit to a user-owned test file, no write to the gate ledger, and no move of a confidence level.

The brief closes with its exit condition. It is complete when the audit is recorded, the missing specifications exist, the live surface drills down and cross-links, and every view re-renders clean — and only then does the pre-registered phase resume. The last line carries the project's rule that a session ends by recording the result and naming the next act.
