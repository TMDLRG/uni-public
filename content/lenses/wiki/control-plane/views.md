This page holds the platform's diagrams, written as text that renders in ordinary viewers with no build step. They are projections of a model kept elsewhere, and the page opens by saying that changing a view here means changing the model too: the model is the record.

The sequence runs from the outside in. A context diagram shows who uses the platform: an operator who enters, operates, authors verdicts and stops runs; an external reviewer who receives evidence and never has write access; and an audience who watches. A container diagram shows the four bodies and the stacks they govern, each label carrying its own law. One whose polled read never spawns anything. One which is the lab, marked partly built, with parts not built and no verdict authored yet. One which projects signals and never scores, ranks or authors. And one which shows and carries and never fabricates. Beneath it the one-way rule: every arrow into the projecting body is a projection, and no arrow out of it actuates anything.

A deployment diagram says which body runs where, with the constraints in the labels. The colony always on one machine, never another. One machine captures the colony but hosts none of it. A third does fan-out only.

The evidence spine is drawn as a flow: registering a gate before the run, a paired run with exactly one differing variable, observation, adversarial review, and authoring a verdict in words rather than a percentage. Then writing a receipt — the record of the run — appending a row that is never edited, and projecting it verbatim. When it was drawn, the first six steps had no home, and the diagram groups them under a heading saying so.

Three state diagrams follow. A gate goes from registered, through pending, to adjudicated. The note beside it says a verdict without a gate registered before the run is refused, and that being first is decided by the commit graph rather than by prose. A line underneath adds that a gate may be lowered on receipts, and that this is the gate working, not a failure. A run may complete, fail, or become void. Rooms progress through grades of cleanliness, needing two keys and, for the strictest, a passing scan; a note says there is no override path.

One diagram is about appearance, and is the most quietly important. It shows the renderer choosing a material from the truth class, never from a style flag, and sending anything without a truth class or receipt reference to fog. Each class gets its own look: solid and lit for the observed, visibly rebuilt for a reconstruction, translucent for a calculation, visibly staged for something simulated. Two rules are written beneath it. No frame rate, glow, motion or particle may imply liveness, which comes only from a real probe, so a frozen world looks frozen even while every process reports up. And passing a gate renders the named behaviour and nothing more: no material, light or room here may depict awareness, experience or life.

A component diagram shows the inside of the body that runs the science, each part labelled by the phase that built it, everything funnelling through a single writer. The closing sequences cover authoring a verdict, admission and release, an airlock with two keys, an emergency stop that is never queued behind an approval, and a disagreement surfaced with both sides carried. The standing instruction: fix the source, not the comparison.
