<!--PLAIN-->

This page documents one screen in the project: a workbench that lets you run the project's own
mathematics in a browser and look inside it. Nothing on that screen is new science. It either
executes code that already exists in the repository, or displays numbers from frozen reports.

The page is unusually honest about what the workbench is not. It is not an experiment, it writes
nothing, and it is not evidence that a real motor performs the calculation. One of its views
runs a synthetic world, so the tiles labelled as observations there are observations of a
simulation.

Two open defects are declared in the middle of the document rather than buried. One equation as
shipped counts a term twice, which changes which action gets chosen, so an equation card
describes a quantity the tool does not produce. And one gate shows a pass while none of its
declared files are present on disk; the page tells you not to read that badge as verified.

<!--CLEAR-->

The document exists because the project's contract requires every surface to declare its
evidence, assumptions, units, falsifier, ledger, reproduction command and limitations. This is
that declaration, for one screen.

It begins by defining the workbench as an instrument that makes committed mathematics executable
and inspectable without re-implementing any of it. Every number it shows comes either from live
execution of existing library code, deterministically and on the processor, or from frozen
report files. It computes no quantity of its own, and a test pins the calculators to the library
functions so a shadow re-implementation cannot creep in.

Then a section on what it is not: not a new experiment, since it runs no fit and writes nothing;
not evidence that a bacterial motor performs the inference, since the agent loop is a reduced
teaching model over a reduced synthetic world; not a claim of biological parity, since the flag
for that is false and is printed on the page. The view that steps through the mathematics runs a
synthetic world, so its observation tiles are observations of the simulation.

A table lists the views: a model stack, a live stepper, a library of equation cards each
declaring its own class, a measured-evidence view, a panel of work planned but not run, and
printable worksheets.

The long middle section covers evidence, units and assumptions. The dwell analysis names its
protocol and its pinned source. The species is stated, and this behavioural evidence is kept
separate from the structural evidence used elsewhere. The experimental unit is the motor, events
are not treated as independent replicates, and all uncertainty comes from resampling motors. A
reconciliation table shows how the source cohort shrinks through each declared exclusion. One
nuance is declared rather than hidden: parameters are fitted on training events only, but the
set of eligible states was chosen using all data.

The held-out result is given with the adverse part in bold. A lognormal baseline out-scores the
project's two-timescale model on point score, and two of the three paired intervals cross zero,
so the page insists the correct wording is that those comparisons are not resolved at this
sample size — not that there is no difference, and not that the baseline wins. Fences carried
over verbatim from the report say that overdispersion does not uniquely identify a molecular
hidden state, and that better prediction does not show the fitted timescales are molecular
states or that the organism performs the inference.

Two known defects are declared inline. The shipped expected free energy adds an epistemic term
twice, because one component is a cross-entropy rather than a divergence; the page gives the
algebra, says the inflation changes action selection, and states that until it is corrected one
equation card and one worksheet describe a quantity the tool does not produce. Separately, a
source-integrity gate renders a pass while none of its declared cached artifacts are present on
disk; the page tells the reader not to read that green badge as verified source integrity.

A list of falsifiers follows — conditions under which the workbench itself would be shown wrong
— each annotated with whether a test protects it. Several are marked unprotected or latent.

The page ends with reproduction commands and a list of limitations: no live browser verification
exists in the audit record; the colour channel that distinguishes pass from fail is
non-functional, because four style properties are used and never declared; no species label
appears on the served page; and the overclaim fences are not rendered.
