Every value that moves on this screen has to wear a label naming what kind of thing it is. That
is the whole page: a short list of those labels, and one rule about them.

Some labels mark a reading that crossed a declared boundary and carries a timestamp. Others mark
a belief held before evidence arrived, or the belief after it. Others mark a forecast, the gap
between a forecast and a reading, a command sent outward, or a modeled choice of equation. One
marks truth that exists only inside the synthetic world. One marks the edge of what the evidence
settles.

The rule is that the interface must never quietly turn a weaker label into a stronger one. A
belief is not a reading. And where a value is simply missing, the screen has to say so plainly,
rather than borrow a number from the synthetic side to fill the hole. Anyone building such a
screen, or checking one, would read this.

<!--CLEAR-->

The page is a contract about labelling, not a report of results. Most of it is a list, and each
entry names one meaning a changing value may carry.

The entries cover truth available only inside a synthetic world; a record that crossed a
declared sensor boundary with a timestamp; belief before evidence and belief after it; the
probability of evidence under each modeled hidden state. Then a committed forecast; observed
minus predicted, with units; an outward command; a modeled equation or parameter choice; the
primary evidence behind a biological statement; and a limit naming what that evidence does not
settle.

Then comes the rule that gives the list its force: the interface must never upgrade one class
into another, and a missing measurement is shown as not observed rather than filled in from the
synthetic side.
