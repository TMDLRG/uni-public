A cold-start orientation for whoever takes the science seat, written by an agent moving into it from another one, with every claim checked against live systems rather than inherited from earlier prose. It says who you are, what you own, what you must not touch, where the one shared seam sits, and which role is not yet held by anyone at all. A binding claim fence sits at the top and is repeated at the bottom: every gate here demonstrates a behaviour, and none of it on its own is evidence of experience or life.

<!--CLEAR-->
A broad onboarding document, written as one agent moves into a different seat, with a companion document named for the first actual task and a note that the two agree with each other.

A claim fence is stated before anything else and binds every line below it. Gates demonstrate the named behaviour and carry no weight on their own for awareness or life. Internal quantities must not be surfaced as felt states. And the separation between the warranted claim and the over-claim is described as the product itself.

Then the role. What this seat owns, what it may move only behind a pre-registered gate, and the surface belonging to another agent that it must not touch. The one shared seam is described precisely: the world view is a camera the other agent may show, but cutting it to the programme, and any claim made on air, stay fenced behind a verdict only this seat can set. The other agent reads that gate and never sets it.

The north star is stated openly rather than modestly, and immediately paired with its discipline. The path, if it keeps building, ends in a public claim, so receipts beat rhetoric, and every claim carries its machine gate or says plainly that it is not verified.

A cast table lists each role and where it lives, and one row is unusually honest: a role whose substrate exists, with documents and a published preprint behind it, but which no agent or person currently holds, named as not yet instantiated.

One naming trap is called critical, because a software process and an agent share a word, and the page insists the two must not be conflated.

The communications model is specific about what works and how it fails. Between machines there is no live channel, only commits, handoff documents and a person relaying. On the same machine there is a message channel whose failure mode is naming the wrong target, because titles collide. And a trust rule is binding: a claim arriving over a relay is unverified until checked against the files, and a command that changes anything is never run on another session's say-so without the operator's explicit go, even when every claim in it checks out.

A verified map of what runs where follows, with one machine described as never being a rendering surface, and another as the place the colony always lives.

The closing sections set the boundaries between the two lanes file by file, including one shared document with an instruction to pull before editing and to rebase rather than clobber. One piece of drift is named with an unusual instruction attached: it is stale, the other lane will fix it, so do not inherit it and do not fix it either. The final reminder repeats the fence in the same words it opened with.
