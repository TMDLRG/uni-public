This is a receipt — a file recording what was run — for a test that came out well, and it is unusually careful about what the good result does not mean. The test asks whether an energy organ actually does anything, using four arms: two where the body can both act and eat, and two where one of those is cut off. The cut-off arms die early. A receipt, the file recording what was run, with a pass verdict, and a banner at the top saying it has since been superseded forward by a live one. An earlier phase had recorded this claim as withheld, because the organ's activation was unverified, and this page is the missing piece of that.

The design is a positive, negative, negative, positive set of arms, and the bars were pinned before the run. How long the good arms must last, how many reversals in the energy estimate count as a genuine limit cycle, and how early the cut-off arms must fail. A results table then gives one row per arm. Both intact arms last the full run with the energy estimate oscillating between depletion and refill. One cut-off arm cannot act at all and drains steadily to death. The other can act but has no real food, and does the same.

The reasoning is that two structurally different negatives rule out a decorative result. Viability depends on acting, and it also depends on food, and the intact arm sustains itself across more than one seed.

The list of what is not claimed, at the end, is as long as the result, which is the point. The scope is mechanism only, in a synthetic world, and the page says plainly not to present it as a production result. It says the finding is silent about a separate behavioural gate that failed live and remains open. And it fences the language: energy here is a model variable, and this is self-maintenance rather than anything felt.
