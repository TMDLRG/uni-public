<!--CLEAR-->
A pre-registration, its conditions written down first, marked as not yet run and explicitly not an authorisation to run. The engine exists and is proven offline, and this document is the artifact for the live gate. That gate needs three separate things before it can happen: some blocking items closed, a signed review of them, and an owner's go-ahead under the live guard.

It is motivated by a recorded refutation. The simpler, reserve-blind shape died in half its worlds and was looser than the hoarding foil it was tested against. The page treats that measured death as the missing depth, and asks whether the added depth earns its place behaviourally rather than merely surviving.

The most important design point is why survival alone cannot be the gate. An offline roll shows the structure survives, holds an interior reserve, paces its activity, and dissociates one subsystem from another. But under a following policy a simpler foil survives too, because both hold food. So the discriminators have to be behavioural, or a foil that just eats more banks the same result. The page names this as the same trap an earlier gate had already pre-registered against.

The arms are decomposed rather than bundled, which is the other design point. Beyond the treatment there is a baseline carrying the shape that died, and a foil that eats to full with no interior peak. Two ablations each remove exactly one of the two mechanisms, so a win can be attributed to a named coupling instead of to the bundle as a whole.

One decision is flagged rather than made quietly. The baseline and foil shapes as they exist today are malformed at the wider cardinality, with a peak stranded low and a supposedly saturating shape that does not actually rise to the ceiling. Defining them properly is new surface, so it must pass review before the launcher is written, and until then the launcher is scaffolded against the treatment alone.

Isolation is applied identically to every arm, dropping one component and pinning a stage, so that nothing re-imports a different set of preferences part-way through the run.

The blocking groups are listed with the property each one preserves, and a theme runs through them. Every new field defaults to something that leaves existing behaviour byte-identical. One quantity is anchored so it can never reach the decision arithmetic. An invariant gate ships in the same change rather than afterwards. And one item is a null-behaviour pre-check, confirming the baseline and the foil behave as expected before the clock starts, which is a control on the controls.

The reviewers' verdicts are recorded individually, each signing with changes, and one of them is blunt that the design was unbuildable as written. The closing line repeats that the ship gate is not cleared, and that the live run needs the owner as well as the review.
