Behind every chapter in this work sits one append-only public record, and this chapter explains what it is for. The program it governs is a developmental active-inference simulation, a bounded peek at a toy world and never a person, so the record exists to hold the wording level with what that simulation actually did. The ledger, not any headline, is the law. No chapter may state a claim above the class recorded there, drop a recorded falsifier, or hide a recorded negative. The load-bearing idea is that negatives are content. A partial result or a recorded failure is a measurement that a design is incomplete; it is not an exit and not something to bury. The clearest example in the chapter is an audit that pulled six of the program's own headline claims down to their measured value: two boxes became one box, a mind surviving a patch became infrastructure continuity only, and five stacks became four distinct stacks over five runs. Nothing was faked. The words had drifted upward and were pulled back.

<!--CLEAR-->

The chapter describes a calibration ledger: a public artifact recording, per claim, an evidence class, a falsifier and one of four verdict states, and ending each chapter with a condition a sceptic could run.

Three rules give the ledger teeth. Calibration only moves wording down to the measured value, never up, including under urgency. A capability verdict is the confidence-interval bound that excludes the threshold, not the point estimate. And done means test-covered, which never satisfies a criterion demanding a runtime observation.

The discipline is described as mechanical rather than well-intentioned. One recorded row describes a server-side evidence contract in which every ticket walks a fixed sequence and each transition is blocked unless an evidence comment is logged first, with a linter that automatically downgrades overclaims. Another records significance as a gate rather than a preference: a result counts when a bootstrap interval on the median paired difference excludes zero under a seeded generator, never on a single seed and never off a point estimate.

The chapter then fences its own headline number. The last recorded ledger snapshot of rows and states is carried as a snapshot with its provenance attached, because the count of negatives cannot be rebuilt from the rows the corpus actually prints.

Its central worked example is an audit that calibrated six headline claims down to what had been measured. Three are load-bearing and must be carried in the corrected form: a clearance on two boxes was really one box; a claim that the mind survives a patch was really infrastructure continuity only; and five stacks was really four distinct stacks over five runs. The continuity claim is carded at its true reach, with a first stage shown across a process restart and a second stage, continuity across a real kernel swap, recorded as not shown and still owed.

The chapter's own summary of the audit is the part worth keeping: nothing was fabricated, the overstatements lived in the summary and headline layers, and the response was to make the fence louder rather than wider.
