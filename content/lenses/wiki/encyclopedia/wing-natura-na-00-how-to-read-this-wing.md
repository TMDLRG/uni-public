Read this one before any other chapter in the wing, because the rest assume it. Its work is to define the vocabulary the wing uses for evidence, and to fence it off from the program's own claim ledger. One hard rule does that: reading somebody else's science contributes no evidence to what the program itself has shown. The chapter asserts no fact about nature on its own account. Every number in it is a worked example, and each arrives with its class, its citation, and the observation that would sink it. The classes separate what many labs corroborated from what a model derived, and both from rows carrying no usable value at all. The chapter also strikes through its own earlier draft. It once declared a six-value vocabulary in those words. Rows written elsewhere in the corpus fitted none of the six, so the vocabulary grew to twelve classes in three groups. The original wording stays on the page, struck rather than quietly deleted.

<!--CLEAR-->

The chapter begins with the cardinal rule: reading somebody else's science contributes no evidence to what the program itself has shown. A scaling law you read in a paper moves nothing in the program's own ledger, and neither does a physics experiment you cite. The two ledgers are cross-referenced by explicit audited link only, never by merge.

It then defines the classes, with a worked example of each. The measured group says how much independent corroboration exists, from replicated across labs, through measured once with no replication on record, to genuinely contested where the community disputes the value or the mechanism and both positions must be carried. The derived group says the assumptions are the fence, covering models that fit data, models that are also disputed, and mechanisms proposed but unsettled. The fenced group carries no usable value and says precisely why not. Some rows failed observation, or cannot be falsified as stated, or were retracted by their own field. Others name a quantity nobody has measured, a value this corpus could not trace, a source named but never read, and a source searched for and not found. The last group's distinctions are load-bearing, because collapsing an untraced value into an unmeasured one would let a research failure masquerade as a discovery about the frontier of science.

Four rows deliberately carry no class at all, and they are named individually as a closed list. There are defining constants, which are conventions rather than observations. There is a prediction, kept as a separate row from the measurement that followed it. There is first-person testimony, which belongs to a different store and is never calibrated. And there is a register entry held outside the ledger because it offers no falsifier.

A crosswalk table then pairs each value of the program's fence with the nature class it superficially resembles, and the last column carries the weight, since it says what each can never imply about the other.

The amendment record is a calibrate-down event on the chapter itself, written openly with the struck wording on record and a later correction showing that the first amendment's scope was narrower than its own account read. The chapter's own fence is that it measures nothing, and its falsifier list is left standing and unweakened.
