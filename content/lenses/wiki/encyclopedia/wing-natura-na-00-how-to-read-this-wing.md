Read this one before any other chapter in the wing, because the rest assume it. Its work is to define the vocabulary the wing uses for evidence, and to keep it apart from the words the program uses about its own claims. One hard rule does that: reading somebody else's science contributes no evidence to what the program itself has shown. The chapter asserts no fact about nature on its own account. Every number in it is a worked example, and each arrives with its class, its citation, and the observation that would sink it. The classes separate what many labs corroborated from what a model derived, and both from rows carrying no usable value at all. The chapter also strikes through its own earlier draft. It once declared a six-value vocabulary in those words. Rows written elsewhere in the corpus fitted none of the six, so the vocabulary grew to twelve classes in three groups. The original wording stays on the page, struck rather than quietly deleted.

<!--CLEAR-->

Reading somebody else's science contributes no evidence to what the program itself has shown. That is the chapter's cardinal rule, and everything else in it is downstream of that one sentence. A scaling law you read in a paper moves nothing in the program's own record of what it has built, and neither does a physics experiment you cite. The two records are cross-referenced by an explicit audited link only, and never merged.

It then defines the classes, with a worked example of each. The measured group says how much independent corroboration exists. It runs from replicated across labs, through measured once with no replication on record, to genuinely contested, where the community disputes the value or the mechanism and both positions must be carried. The derived group says the assumptions are the limit, covering models that fit data, models that are also disputed, and mechanisms proposed but unsettled. The third group carries no usable value and says precisely why not. Some rows failed observation, or cannot be sunk by any observation as stated, or were retracted by their own field. Others name a quantity nobody has measured, a value this corpus could not trace to a source, a source named but never read, and a source searched for and not found. Those last distinctions are load-bearing. Collapsing an untraced value into an unmeasured one would let a research failure masquerade as a discovery about the frontier of science.

Four rows deliberately carry no class at all, and they are named individually as a closed list. There are defining constants, which are conventions rather than observations. There is a prediction, kept as a separate row from the measurement that followed it. There is first-person testimony, which belongs to a different store and is never calibrated. And there is a register entry deliberately kept out of the wing's own list, because it offers nothing that could show it wrong.

A crosswalk table then pairs each value of the program's own status vocabulary with the nature class it superficially resembles. The last column carries the weight, since it says what each can never imply about the other.

The amendment record is a calibrate-down event on the chapter itself, written openly with the struck wording on record and a later correction showing that the first amendment's scope was narrower than its own account read. The chapter says of itself that it measures nothing, and it leaves standing, unweakened, its own list of the results that would show it wrong.
