A reference work about a research program called UNI opens here. What you get is an index rather than an argument: a list of the chapters, and the rules they were written under. The program described is a simulation of development, a small toy world, never a person and never a mind. Its honest position is printed without softening. Only about two of eleven or more developmental steps are earned, and everything else is parked, negative, pending or fenced. Two other files govern all of it, a ledger of claims and an authoring plan. Where a chapter and the ledger disagree, the ledger wins and the chapter is wrong. The lines the work will not cross are listed too, including any claim of human-level intelligence, of awareness, or of having created life.

The UNI Encyclopedia is a public reference work about a developmental active-inference simulation, and this page is its table of contents. It makes no scientific claim of its own; it tells you where the claims live and what discipline they were written under.

The rule it repeats hardest is that every chapter is authored against two other files and never ahead of them. One is an append-only claim ledger, where each claim carries an evidence class, a condition that would show it false, and one of four states: pass, fail, negative or pending. The other is an authoring blueprint holding the front-matter rules, the class rubric, the standing fences and the per-chapter specification. If prose and ledger ever disagree, the ledger wins.

The index then lays out the wings. An evidence wing explains how to read the work and describes the calibration record. A science wing walks a developmental ladder from a modelled zygote up through cells, metabolism, an organ lab, affect, movement, perception, language and self-model, and then names the rungs above that as parked or not yet built. A meaning wing carries the free energy principle as a lens only. A movement wing describes the honest-science posture and the public invitation to falsify. A method wing carries the reusable disciplines. An appendix covers a marketing and publishing organism, carded as engineering, never as a capability.

The standing red lines are printed rather than implied: no claim of general or human-level intelligence, no claim of consciousness or sentience, no claim that active inference has been shown, no claim that life or measurable awareness has been created, and no claim of beating large language models. Every chapter is required to publish the negative that travels with each pass, and to end with a falsifier a reader could run.
