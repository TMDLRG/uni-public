The UNI Encyclopedia is a public reference work about a developmental active-inference simulation, and this page is its table of contents. It makes no scientific claim of its own; it tells you where the claims live and what discipline they were written under.

The rule it repeats hardest is that every chapter is authored against two other files and never ahead of them. One is a claim ledger, a list that rows are added to and never edited. Each claim in it carries an evidence class, a condition that would show it false, and one of four states: pass, fail, negative or pending. The other is an authoring blueprint holding the front-matter rules, the class rubric, the standing limits and the per-chapter specification. If prose and ledger ever disagree, the ledger wins.

The index then lays out the wings. An evidence wing explains how to read the work and describes the calibration record. A science wing walks a developmental ladder from a modelled zygote up through cells, metabolism, an organ lab, affect, movement, perception, language and self-model, and then names the rungs above that as parked or not yet built. A meaning wing carries the free energy principle as a lens only. A movement wing describes the honest-science posture and the public invitation to falsify. A method wing carries the reusable disciplines. An appendix covers a marketing and publishing organism, recorded as engineering, never as a capability.

The standing red lines are printed rather than implied. There is no claim of general or human-level intelligence, and none of consciousness or sentience. There is no claim that active inference has been shown, none that life or measurable awareness has been created, and none of beating large language models. Every chapter is required to publish the negative that travels with each pass, and to end by naming the result that would show the chapter wrong.
