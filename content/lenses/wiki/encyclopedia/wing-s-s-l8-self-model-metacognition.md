No step on the developmental ladder invites a bigger overclaim than this one. The simulation records a passing report card across a battery of gates for self-modelling and metacognition. Functional is the operative word, and it means something narrow and testable: the simulation keeps and uses a model of its own states, and it can watch and adjust its own processing on registered tasks. It does not mean experience, feeling or an inner life. Phenomenal sentience is disclaimed outright, and the chapter is careful about what sort of statement that is. Sentience was not tested and failed here; it was disclaimed. So there is no gate for it, no interval, and no result that could overturn it. Two negatives travel with the pass and belong beside it in every citation.

<!--CLEAR-->

The single recorded claim is a maturation report card passing across a list of gates: immersion, affect, growth, self-model, metacognition, metalinguistics, consolidation, reflective and compositional readers, and online vocabulary growth. On the strength of that suite the program asserts functional self-awareness.

Two things must be read precisely. The tally is over a set of gates registered in advance, so they could not be chosen afterwards, and each carries its own held verdict. It is not a single grand metric of selfhood. And the class it sits at is the empirical pass tier in this corpus, but the verdict is functional rather than phenomenal. It certifies that the modelled gates do what the model says under their registered evaluations, and nothing about inner experience.

What would show the claim wrong has three prongs. Re-run the held battery, and if any gate flips its registered verdict the tally is false. Take any self-model or metacognition gate and apply its discriminator, shuffling the labels, swapping the markers or ablating the term to zero, and if the gain collapses then that gate was not measuring metacognition. And if a self-model task turns out to be passable by a trivial heuristic with no metacognitive content, the label is unearned. What is deliberately absent is any test that could overturn a claim of sentience, and that absence is not an oversight.

Two negatives are mandatory companions. The first is the disclaimer itself, treated as a first-class limit rather than a footnote. The second is in genuine tension with the pass, which is exactly why they are co-cited: the report card comes from the developmental-simulation side, while on the language-reader side a strong self-model capability was attempted and held negative. These are not the same artifact, on the same substrate, at the same task, and they must never be merged into a single triumphant line.

The chapter treats that pairing as the credibility of this step on the ladder. A reader who sees only the tally would conclude far more than the evidence supports. A reader who sees it beside the held negative sees the real shape: a passed functional battery on one substrate, a failed strong-capability attempt on another, and an explicit disclaimer over the whole region.
