One result in the whole program is ready to be put in front of an outside examiner, and this chapter is about that result. A reader built only from counts, with no gradient step anywhere in it, beats a tuned baseline on real text that was sealed away and scored once. It is exactly one thing: a count-baseline win. It is not active inference shown, not comprehension, not talking, and not a win over large language models, since by a chosen design trade the same program sits behind gradient-trained models on character perplexity. The program around it stays a developmental active-inference simulation, a bounded peek at a toy world and never a person. The pass is real, its ceiling is fixed, and the chapter prints the two negatives that bound it on either side. Of many structural designs tried, only the cache wins. And the gain turned out diffuse, so there was nothing left for a controller to gate.

<!--CLEAR-->

The reader is built from exact count statistics and a multi-level recency cache. It lives inside a simulation: a toy world, and there is no person in it. Its only learning is conjugate count addition, with no automatic differentiation, no gradient step and no trained network. It is evaluated against a tuned smoothing baseline on real text that was sealed away and scored once, and the baseline is properly tuned rather than a strawman, because the discipline requires a tuned opponent before any margin is claimed.

The verdict is licensed by the lower bound of a multi-seed interval, not by the point estimate, and that bound clears the bar that was set before the run, comfortably. The result is checked by three gates and earned the first genuinely validator-derived reproduction flag in the ledger, a record added to and never edited. That flag comes from several distinct seeds and a real, non-degenerate interval rather than being written in by hand. One warning about where a figure came from travels with it, since a separate replication row records a different margin and interval, and the two are distinct measurements that must never be quoted for each other.

A family of supporting held results corroborates the headline one, each bounded the same way. One of them matters most for interpretation, because it beats an unbounded backbone and so closes the common deflationary reading that the baseline simply had too short a window. A recency ablation removes a measurable part of the win, which is what a true ablation should do.

Alongside the language spine sits a set of test-covered, parity-checked precision labs that trace a two-dimensional map into distinct behavioural regimes. They illustrate the engine, and they are never a comprehension claim.

Two negatives are mandatory companions. One records that of several structurally distinct within-segment designs, all were held negative and only the cache wins, so the published bound is scoped to the tested envelope and is not a universal impossibility. The other records that two active-controller families were built to gate the gain and both showed the gain is diffuse, with nothing to gate.

A registered working law explains the pattern: a representation trained without gradients beats a tuned baseline only when it carries conditional information the baseline lacks, and only when that representation is stable enough that the gain exceeds its estimation cost. The bounds are the same law read the other way.
