The free energy principle, taught at textbook level, is the lens the encyclopedia uses to organise everything else. A lens is not a verdict. Nothing here claims that active inference is the correct theory of mind, or that it has been shown inside the program. The program itself is a simulation, a toy world rather than a person. The plainest idea is that an organism keeps its predictions and its observations in line either by changing the prediction or by changing the world, and the chapter teaches this with a ladder of everyday examples. It also carries a correction it is careful not to lose: minimising variational free energy does not reduce surprise that has already happened. It tightens an upper bound on it by improving an approximate posterior. That correction came from a human reviewer who would not sign off on the first draft, and the chapter treats where it came from as content.

<!--CLEAR-->

This is a meaning chapter. It asserts a framing and no capability. The program remains a developmental simulation, a bounded peek into a toy world.

The lens is taught from four everyday examples that climb the abstraction, and the unifying idea is that inferring the hidden causes is the perception half while acting to make predictions come true is the active half. Recorded as a governance pattern, the lens can be reused but cannot be raised into an empirical claim.

A note travels with it and must never be stripped. That nobody can falsify a statement is not evidence for it. An unfalsifiable statement sits outside science by definition, so the very breadth that makes this principle feel all-explaining is a liability rather than a credential. The claimed strength is the opposite: a checked core with a frontier marked off rather than claimed.

The lens is given structure by a three-layer framing built from two typed boundaries, in which hardware signals enter as interoception and the mind runs a discrete loop of perceiving, planning, acting and learning, with no gradient step. The framing carries its own limits inline. The composed appliance is described as variationally controlled and exact only at a single-step interface, not globally exact, and recording it as globally exact is the violation on file.

The most-misread point gets its own section. For a fixed observed outcome the surprisal is what it is; minimising free energy tightens an upper bound on it by moving the approximate posterior toward the true one, with equality exactly when the two coincide. Perception tightens the bound; action lives one layer up. Stating that minimisation reduces already-observed surprise falsifies the chapter by definition.

A final piece covers a bookkeeping rule for hierarchies: a level must never treat an upstream prior as if it were fresh evidence, so the prior is divided out. A cheaper factored approximation was implemented, measured as lossy, and rejected, and that rejection is recorded rather than glossed.

The chapter closes with what it does not claim, and with the observation that what is owed sits one level out, in the science it frames.
