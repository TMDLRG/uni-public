The encyclopedia is not written here; the rules for writing it are. Every chapter inherits its front matter from this file: the evidence constitution, the lettered class rubric, and the standing limits and red lines. Each also inherits a template for the section where a chapter states what it does not claim. Then come the body structure, a one-page specification per chapter, and an ordered writing queue. Two rules run through all of it. A chapter authors against the claim ledger, a list rows are added to and never edited, and never ahead of it. Where the two disagree the ledger wins. A chapter that cites a positive result without the negative bounding it has overclaimed, and fails review. The program described throughout is a developmental simulation, with roughly two of eleven or more developmental steps earned.

<!--CLEAR-->

Everything in this file is about how to write, not about what was found. It is a plan: it reports no scientific result of its own, and instead fixes the conditions under which a result may be written up at all. The program it plans for is a developmental simulation, and every chapter is told to say so in the same words.

Its first part is the constitution the chapters inherit. Six rules govern every entry. Every claim must name the result that would show it wrong. One ledger is the source of truth, and rows are added to it rather than edited. Calibration only moves wording down toward the measured value. A verdict is read off the interval bound rather than the point estimate. Test-covered and working are held strictly apart, and negatives are treated as content. A note beside the headline count of published negatives says where that figure came from, because it is a recorded snapshot and cannot be rebuilt from the rows the corpus prints. A no-exit discipline says the only honest place to stop is a working solution or a published, falsifiable bound, and that a parked question is not discharged until its sign-off lands.

Next comes the class rubric, from a machine-exact anchor down to a class meaning claimed but unearned, with the reminder that a passing test never satisfies a criterion demanding a runtime observation. An exactness rule forbids recording a lower-precision result at a higher-precision tier, and notes that a docstring once did exactly that and was corrected. Then the red lines, listed one by one, and a template every chapter must fill in to say what it is not claiming.

The second part is structure. A table of contents runs across an evidence wing, a science ladder, a meaning wing, a movement wing, a method wing and a marketing appendix. One short specification per chapter names its class, the result that would show it wrong, and the negative that travels with it.

The third part is an ordered writing queue with standing rules attached. No chapter ships without its block saying what is not claimed. No row may be recorded at a higher evidence class than its ledger row allows. And a positive result may not be written without the negative beside it in the same pass.
