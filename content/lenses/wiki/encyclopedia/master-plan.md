The encyclopedia is not written here; the rules for writing it are. Every chapter inherits its front matter from this file: the evidence constitution, the lettered class rubric, and the standing fences and red lines. Each also inherits a template for the section where a chapter states what it does not claim. Then come the body structure, a one-page specification per chapter, and an ordered writing queue. Two rules run through all of it. A chapter authors against the claim ledger and never ahead of it, and where the two disagree the ledger wins. A chapter that cites a positive result without the negative bounding it has overclaimed, and fails review. The program described throughout is a developmental simulation, with roughly two of eleven or more rungs earned.

<!--CLEAR-->

Everything in this file is about how to write, not about what was found. It is a plan: it reports no scientific result of its own, and instead fixes the conditions under which a result may be written up at all. The program it plans for is a developmental simulation, and every chapter is told to say so in the same words.

Its first part is the constitution the chapters inherit. Six rules govern every entry: a falsifier for every claim, one append-only ledger as the source of truth, calibration that only moves wording down toward the measured value, a verdict read off the interval bound rather than the point estimate, a strict split between test-covered and working, and negatives treated as content. A provenance fence sits beside the headline count of published negatives, because that figure is a recorded snapshot and cannot be rebuilt from the rows the corpus prints. A no-exit discipline says the only honest place to stop is a working solution or a published, falsifiable bound, and that a parked question is not discharged until its sign-off lands.

Next comes the class rubric, from a machine-exact anchor down to a class meaning claimed but unearned, with the reminder that a passing test never satisfies a criterion demanding a runtime observation. An exactness rule forbids carding a lower-precision result at a higher-precision tier, and records that a docstring once did exactly that and was corrected. Then the red lines, listed one by one, and a template every chapter must fill in to say what it is not claiming.

The second part is structure: a table of contents across an evidence wing, a science ladder, a meaning wing, a movement wing, a method wing and a marketing appendix, with one short specification per chapter naming its class, its falsifier and its travelling negative.

The third part is an ordered writing queue with standing rules attached, including that no chapter ships without its not-claimed block, that no row may be carded above its ledger class, and that a positive result may not be written without the negative beside it in the same pass.
