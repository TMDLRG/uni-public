Everything else in the encyclopedia is governed by the rules set out here, and this is the one chapter claiming no capability at all. It describes how each claim is graded, what would show it false, and which rules the authors may not break. Read it as a contract, not a result. The program behind it is a developmental simulation that grows from a modelled zygote toward a modelled speaking child, and it is never a person and never a mind. The chapter is blunt that a disciplined ledger is not the same thing as a correct science. What a good ledger buys you is narrower. When something is shown, you will be able to tell, because it will sit at a stated evidence class beside a stated falsifier and the negatives that travel with it.

<!--CLEAR-->

The evidence constitution is set out here, and the chapter then insists on its own limits. It asserts no capability, and it says so first. What sits underneath it is a developmental simulation, so nothing in the constitution is offered as a scientific result.

Six rules carry the constitution. Every claim ships with a stated condition that would show it false; a claim without one is marketing and is forbidden. One append-only ledger is the source of truth, with four states only, and corrections are made by superseding a row with its lineage rather than editing it. Calibration only moves wording down toward the measured value, including under pressure. A verdict is the interval bound that excludes the threshold, not the point estimate. Done means test-covered, which is not the same as working. And negative results are content, not something to hide.

The chapter then explains the class ladder. Classes run from a live runtime observation, through code inspection or a pre-registered held-out gate, through a passing test suite, to an inherited document that must be re-checked before it is leaned on, plus a class meaning claimed but unearned and a class for governance patterns. The class is a ceiling: a chapter may card a claim at or below it, never above. Two worked examples make the ceiling concrete, including a case where a route was covered by tests yet had never fired in the running system.

Further sections cover how conflicting sources are resolved by class rather than by confidence, and the discipline of registering a bar before building, touching a held-out set exactly once, and deriving a reproduction flag from multiple seeds rather than writing it in by hand. That rule exists because it was once violated and caught.

A short section fences the corpus's own favourite statistic. The recorded count of published negatives is a snapshot, not a figure a reader can rebuild from the rows on show, so it must be cited as a snapshot.

The chapter closes with what it does not claim and with a falsifier a reader can run: find one published claim stated above its ledger class, carrying no falsifier, calibrated upward, or with a verdict quietly edited, and the chapter's own claim fails.
