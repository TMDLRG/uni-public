Every number on this page came out of somebody else's work, quoted here, so the page contributes no evidence to what the programme has built. It also lists what it does not claim about itself, and the sharpest item is that the ladder is not a progression, a hierarchy of value, or a direction. What it lays out is the wing's spine: a rung-by-rung table from something very small to something very large. The real payload is a rule for knowing when a number fitted on one rung stops meaning anything on the next. The picture to drop is the ladder as a smooth continuum with things sprinkled along it. It is a set of regimes with boundaries between them, and the boundaries are where the engineering value sits. Stay inside a regime and one force dominates, a group or two governs, and a fitted power law behaves. Cross a boundary and the dominant force changes identity, so any exponent carried across is a category error wearing a decimal point. The chapter classes itself as modelled for exactly that reason. Read the boundary column down the table and it does not rise with size. Some very large things have no boundary and some very small ones do. Size is not what makes one.

Nothing here was measured by the programme; the chapter quotes published work, and contributes no evidence to the programme's own results. Its table places each rung with a characteristic length, a characteristic time belonging to that rung's own dynamics, and a characteristic energy. It adds the dominant force or constraint, the governing dimensionless groups, and whether a boundary of the kind an earlier chapter defines is identifiable there.

A rung is then defined operably. Three conditions must all hold, or you have named a size rather than identified a level. A boundary with real conditional independence forms. A new characteristic timescale separates from the level below, which is the actual mechanism of hierarchy, and comes with a classic argument that stable intermediates are what interruption spares, so hierarchies are what evolution can reach. And the level has its own dynamics that do not require tracking the level below. The chapter notes the honest ordering, since the second condition causes the third, and that emergence here is a statement about spectral gaps in a dynamical system rather than about magic.

The best regime-change example is the ratio of inertial to viscous forces across self-propelled organisms, spanning many more orders of magnitude than the figure usually in circulation, corrected here rather than carried. The physics of swimming changes identity across it, since at the small end a swimmer cannot coast at all and a reciprocal stroke nets nothing. The chapter then shows the theorem is premise-bound, because a single-hinge device does propel in non-Newtonian fluids. The theorem was not refuted; its premise was withdrawn.

A second example takes the wing's most-quoted scaling law and shows the exponent changing at major evolutionary boundaries, from superlinear to linear to sublinear, which is a rung boundary showing up in the residuals. A curvature result inside one regime is carried as contested, with both turns of the published exchange printed.

The operable output is a rule with a detection procedure. A power law fitted within a regime does not extrapolate through a regime boundary, and five checks are given to run before extrapolating an exponent across any decade you did not measure.

The gradients are then sorted into the ones with a measurand behind them and the ones without, and the chapter is hardest on the gradient a reader would most like to have. Whether anything like a degree of internal model rises along the ladder has no measurand at all, and it is recorded as not measured rather than estimated. The asymmetry between what the ladder invites and what stands behind it is stated on purpose.

The chapter is classed as modelled overall, and it closes with a list of things it does not claim about itself. Not that the ladder is a progression, a hierarchy of value or a direction, since it is an ordering by length and one measure of specific power runs the other way along it. Not that emergence is mysterious, and not that it is trivial, both readings being defects. Not that a framework of major transitions is beyond debate. Not that one published exchange about curvature has a winner, since both turns are carried and neither is the verdict. Not that its chosen rungs are the only defensible carving. And the standing one: the chapter contributes no evidence to the programme's own results, because the vocabulary for nature's regularities and the vocabulary for its build status describe different kinds of claim and never merge.
